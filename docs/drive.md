## What it actually does

You sign in with Google, in Google's own window, and the app receives permission to use Google Drive.
From then on your gradebook is copied into your Drive whenever it changes, alongside the copy on your computer.
Sign in on a second computer and your gradebook is there.

The copy goes to your Google Drive and nowhere else.
There is no server of ours in the middle, because there is no server of ours.
Your students' names and scores are never sent to us, and we could not read them if we wanted to.

## What Greatbook is allowed to see

The permission asked for is the narrowest one Google offers, called `drive.file`.
It means the app can only see files it created itself, plus any file you specifically hand it with Google's own file picker.

It cannot list your Drive.
It cannot open your other documents, your photos, or anything a colleague shared with you.
Those files are not hidden from it by politeness; Google does not give them to it.

That is also why signing in is ordinary and quick.
Broader Drive permissions would put your whole Drive in reach, and Google would rightly make the sign-in screen say so.

## Worth knowing before you buy

You need a Google account, and your key is tied to it.
That is what keeps the key yours, and Drive backup needs a Google account in any case.

Greatbook checks the key when you first paste it, and once in a while after that, so it needs the internet occasionally.
If you are offline for a fortnight it carries on regardless; past that, Drive backup pauses until the app can ask again, and nothing has to be bought a second time.
Everything else in Greatbook works with no connection at all.

Many districts turn off third-party apps for school Google accounts.
If yours has, Greatbook notices and simply does not offer Drive, rather than offering it and then failing when you take it up.
If that is your situation, this is not worth buying, and the app will say so rather than take your money quietly.

## Turning it off

Two ways, and both work.

In the app, Settings, under Sync, has a switch for Google Drive.
Turning it off stops the copying.
The file already in your Drive is yours and is left where it is; delete it from Drive if you want it gone.

You can also withdraw the permission from Google's side, at [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions), which cuts off the app's access whatever the app thinks.

Everything else in the app works exactly the same afterwards.
Drive is an addition to Greatbook, not something it depends on.
